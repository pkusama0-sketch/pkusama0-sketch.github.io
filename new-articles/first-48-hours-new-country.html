What to do in your first 48 hours in a new country

The first two days can feel busy. Focus on safety, communication and the essentials before trying to solve everything at once.

1. Get to your first accommodation safely

Use transport you can identify and understand before arrival. Save the address offline, keep your phone charged and let a trusted person know you have arrived. If a booking looks different from its listing or someone pressures you to pay more on arrival, pause and contact the booking platform.

2. Make sure you can communicate

Connect to trusted Wi-Fi or arrange a local SIM or eSIM from a recognised provider. Download an offline map of your area and save the address of your accommodation in the local language if needed. Keep emergency numbers in your phone as well as on paper.

3. Secure your documents and money

Carry your passport and other required documents securely. Store copies separately from originals. Avoid showing large amounts of cash, and use official bank machines or recognised currency-exchange services where possible.

4. Learn your immediate area

Find a grocery shop, pharmacy, transport stop and safe route back to your accommodation in daylight. Notice local opening hours and any building rules. Do not feel pressured to accept help, rides or accommodation offers from strangers.

5. Check your official next steps

Some visitors or new residents must register after arrival. Check the official immigration authority, embassy or local government website for your own status. Requirements change, so do not rely only on social-media posts.

Start small: rest, eat, charge your devices and plan tomorrow. A calm first day is more useful than trying to complete every task immediately.
