How to make a simple emergency plan before travelling

You cannot predict every problem on a trip, but you can make the first few decisions easier. A small emergency plan is useful for lost documents, missed transport, illness, phone problems or unexpected changes.

Save the important numbers

Keep local emergency numbers, your accommodation, travel provider, insurance contact and relevant embassy or consulate details in your phone. Write the most important numbers on paper as a backup.

Keep copies of key documents

Store secure copies of your passport information, visa or permit documents, insurance details, bookings and important identification. Keep copies separate from the originals and use a protected account for digital storage.

Plan for a dead or lost phone

Carry a charging cable and consider a suitable power bank. Know the address of your accommodation and at least one route there without relying entirely on your phone. If you use a travel eSIM, keep activation details somewhere safe.

Decide who should know your plans

Give a trusted person your basic itinerary and accommodation details when appropriate. Tell them how they can reach you and what to do if they cannot contact you for an unusually long time.

Prepare for lost cards or cash

Know how to contact your bank or card provider from abroad. Keep payment methods separate rather than putting every card and all cash in one place. Do not share passwords or security codes with anyone claiming to help.

Know the local official resources

Before departure, identify the official government, embassy, transport and healthcare information sources relevant to your trip. Save the websites or contact pages so you do not have to search from scratch during a stressful situation.

Keep the plan short

A useful emergency plan should be easy to read in a hurry. Put the most important contacts, document locations and immediate steps on one page. Review it before departure and update anything that has changed.
