How to choose a phone plan or eSIM when travelling abroad

Your phone can be essential for maps, bookings, two-factor authentication and contacting people after arrival. Comparing the right details before you leave can prevent avoidable connectivity problems.

Decide what you actually need

Estimate how many days you will be away and whether you mainly need maps and messaging or also video, hotspot use and frequent calls. A light user may need much less data than someone working remotely.

Compare roaming with local options

Check your home carrier's international roaming terms first. Then compare reputable local SIM or eSIM providers. Look at total cost, data allowance, validity period, calling options and whether the plan can be renewed.

Check phone compatibility

Before buying an eSIM or physical SIM, confirm that your phone supports the required option and is not restricted to a particular carrier. Follow the device manufacturer's instructions for installing or switching plans.

Keep your main number accessible

If your home number is used for important account verification, plan how you will receive those messages while abroad. Do not disable a service you still need without checking its consequences first.

Download essentials before departure

Save offline maps, booking confirmations, important addresses and emergency contacts. This gives you a fallback if the new connection takes time to activate or coverage is temporarily unavailable.

Protect your account information

Only buy plans from providers you can verify. Avoid sharing account passwords or unnecessary personal information with sellers. Keep your phone protected with a screen lock and use multi-factor authentication where appropriate.

Review the plan after arrival

Check that the data connection, billing and expiry date match what you purchased. If something looks different, contact the provider through its official support channel rather than an unofficial message or social-media account.
