How to avoid rental scams when moving abroad

Finding housing in another country can be stressful, especially when you are under time pressure. A few verification steps can help you spot risky listings before you pay a deposit.

Be cautious about pressure

Treat urgent demands such as 'pay today or lose the property' as a reason to slow down. A legitimate opportunity can still have deadlines, but you should have enough time to understand the agreement, payment terms and identity of the person or company involved.

Verify the property and listing

Compare the address, photos, description and contact details across reliable sources. If possible, use an independent map or local property platform to check whether the location appears genuine. Be especially careful when photos look copied or the address cannot be verified.

Check who is asking for money

Ask for the landlord, agent or company's full name and contact details. For an agency, look for an independently verifiable business presence. Do not assume that a profile, logo or copied identification document proves ownership.

Read the agreement before paying

Make sure the contract clearly states the property, rental period, deposit, rent, utilities, cancellation terms and conditions for returning the deposit. If you cannot understand an important clause, get qualified local help before signing.

Use safer payment practices

Avoid sending money through unusual payment methods simply because someone says it is faster. Confirm the recipient and payment instructions independently. Keep receipts, messages and copies of the agreement in one place.

Have a backup plan

If you are arriving from another country, consider short-term accommodation that gives you time to inspect longer-term housing in person. A few extra days of flexibility may be cheaper than losing a large deposit to a fraudulent listing.

Know when to walk away

If the person refuses reasonable verification, changes the price unexpectedly, asks for sensitive information too early or discourages you from reading the contract, stop and reassess. No listing is worth ignoring basic safety checks.
