How to use public Wi-Fi more safely when travelling

Public Wi-Fi is useful for maps and messages, but it is not the best place for sensitive tasks. A few habits can lower your risk.

Confirm the network name

Ask a staff member for the exact Wi-Fi name before joining. Scammers can create networks with names that look similar to a café, airport or hotel. If a network asks for unusual information or a payment card just to connect, do not continue.

Save banking and important logins for later

Avoid banking, shopping payments, password changes and private work on public Wi-Fi if you can. Use your mobile connection instead, or wait until you are on a network you trust. Always check that the website address is correct before signing in.

Use secure settings

Keep your phone and browser updated. Turn off automatic connection to open Wi-Fi networks and turn off file sharing when you are not using it. If a site offers multi-factor authentication, enable it before travelling.

Log out on shared devices

Never sign into important accounts on a public computer unless absolutely necessary. If you do, use private browsing, do not save passwords and log out completely when you finish.

Watch for urgent messages

Messages claiming that your account is locked, a payment failed or a document is urgently required often try to rush you. Open the official app or type the organisation's address yourself instead of using a link in the message.

These steps cannot remove every risk, but they make your online routine much safer while travelling.
